# Path of the Fleshweaver

Fleshweavers harvest the remains of fallen creatures to build and refine specialized undead servants. Their stitchwork minions are adaptable creations, enhanced with grafts and necrotic energy.

## Tool Proficiency

_3rd-level Path of the Fleshweaver feature_

You gain proficiency with leatherworker's tools. If you already have this proficiency, you gain proficiency with one other type of artisan's tools of your choice.

## Stitchwork Minion

_3rd-level Path of the Fleshweaver feature_

You can craft specialized Undead servants called stitchwork minions from corpses. These minions are friendly to you and your companions. Their statistics appear below; they use your proficiency bonus (PB) in several places.

You can control a number of stitchwork minions equal to half your proficiency bonus (rounded down). If you create another minion over your maximum, one minion of your choice is destroyed. You share a telepathic link with your minions while they remain within 120 feet of you. Through this link, you can issue commands to any number of your minions as a bonus action, giving individual commands or one command to all of them.

In combat, your stitchwork minions share your initiative count, and each takes its turn after yours. A minion follows your most recent command until it completes the task, receives a new command, or can no longer fulfill the order. If it has no command it can meaningfully act on, it takes the Dodge action.

If a minion dies, it crumbles to dust after 1 hour and is destroyed. Until it is destroyed, you can reanimate it using your leatherworker's tools. Spend 1 minute within 5 feet of the minion and expend a 1st-level or higher spell slot; it is reanimated, regains all its hit points, and returns under your control.

## Fleshweaving

_3rd-level Path of the Fleshweaver feature_

You can harvest body parts from corpses and graft them onto your stitchwork minions. Each minion can bear one graft at 3rd level, two grafts at 6th level, and three grafts at 10th level. See [[#Stitchwork Minion Crafting]] below.

### Corpse Satchel

As part of your fleshweaving rituals, you can craft a special extradimensional container called a corpse satchel. It opens a subdimensional fold within the Mortis Veil, accessible only to you, and stores remains for future minion creation or grafting.

You must spend 1 hour crafting the satchel with leatherworker's tools; you can do so during a short or long rest. It takes the form of a leather bag, satchel, pack, or other container of your choice. At the GM's discretion, only corpses and harvested body parts can be stored inside. It can hold up to 500 pounds of these materials, suspended in time and insulated from decay. At 13th level, you can craft a satchel that holds up to 1,000 pounds.

You can retrieve or store one corpse or part as an action. If the satchel is destroyed or lost, its contents are shunted into the Void and lost forever. You can have only one functioning corpse satchel at a time. If it is destroyed or replaced, all contents are instantly lost and unusable.

## Mortis Enhancement

_6th-level Path of the Fleshweaver feature_

You can channel necrotic energy into your creations to make them specialized battlefield threats.

### Augmentation

As a bonus action, expend 2 mortis points to empower a stitchwork minion for 1 minute with one of the following augmentations:

- **Artillerist.** Arcane energy builds behind the minion's gaze. It gains the following action:
  - **Arcane Bolt.** _Ranged Spell Attack:_ your spell attack modifier to hit, range 60 feet, one target. _Hit:_ 2 × PB force damage.
- **Brute.** The minion grows bristling armor of spikes and bone, gaining a +2 bonus to AC.
- **Soldier.** When the minion takes the Attack action, it can make an additional attack as a bonus action.
- **Stalker.** The minion's walking speed increases by 10 feet, and it can take the Dash or Disengage action as a bonus action on each of its turns.

A minion can benefit from only one augmentation at a time; applying a new one ends the previous effect.

### Healing

When you cast a spell that deals necrotic damage, you can choose to target your stitchwork minions or affect minions within the spell's area of effect. Instead of taking damage, each affected minion regains hit points equal to half the necrotic damage the spell would have dealt, ignoring resistances and immunities.

Starting at 13th level, when you use this feature, you can expend 2 mortis points per affected minion to restore hit points equal to the full amount of necrotic damage the spell would have dealt to it, instead of half.

## Master Fleshweaving

_10th-level Path of the Fleshweaver feature_

### Mortis Bond

Your newly crafted minions gain the following benefits:

- They add your proficiency bonus to any ability check or saving throw they make.
- When they deal necrotic damage, they ignore resistance to necrotic damage and treat immunity as resistance.

Additionally, when a minion hits with or is hit by an attack, it can use its reaction to enable you to use a Veil Technique that requires a reaction, using the minion as the source of the technique, even if you have used a Veil Technique this turn. You must expend 2 mortis points in addition to the technique's normal cost. After using a technique through a minion this way, you can't do so again until the start of your next turn.

### Enhanced Augmentation

When you use Augmentation, you can expend 3 additional mortis points (5 total) to extend its duration to 1 hour. You can extend a duration this way a number of times equal to your Intelligence modifier (minimum once), regaining all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

Additionally, when you craft a stitchwork minion or over the course of a long rest, you can use one of its available graft slots to permanently enhance it with one augmentation from Augmentation. This effect is always active until the minion is destroyed. While permanently augmented, the minion can't benefit from that same augmentation again, and one of its available graft slots is permanently used. A minion can have only one permanent augmentation in this way.

## Wraithbound Army

_18th-level Path of the Fleshweaver feature_

As an action, expend mortis points to transform up to three of your stitchwork minions into wraithbound minions for 1 minute, turning their flesh into spectral essence. The cost is 2 mortis points per minion. Each transformed minion gains the following benefits:

- **Death Wail.** When the minion hits a creature with a melee weapon attack, it can use a bonus action to force that creature to make a Wisdom saving throw against your spell save DC. On a failed save, the creature is frightened until the end of its next turn. On a successful save, or after the duration ends for it, the creature is immune to Death Wail for 24 hours.
- **Feed on Fear.** The minion has advantage on attacks against frightened creatures, and its attacks deal an extra PB psychic damage on a hit against them.
- **Phantom Limbs.** The minion can move through other creatures and objects as if they were difficult terrain. If it ends its turn inside a creature or object, it takes 1d10 force damage and is shunted to the last unoccupied space it left. It ignores nonmagical difficult terrain, and opportunity attacks against it have disadvantage.

Once you use this feature, you can't use it again until you finish a long rest, unless you spend 6 mortis points to use it again.

### Minion grafts

Each grafted body part grants an improvement or trait reflecting its source creature. Harvesting and grafting are separate procedures.

#### Harvesting parts

You can harvest a body part only from creature types and sizes from which you can craft stitchwork minions. You can harvest an arm, leg, wings, tail, or head. Spend 1 minute using leatherworker's tools to separate the part from the corpse and infuse it with energy from the Mortis Veil. Then make an Intelligence (Arcana) or Wisdom (Medicine) check with a DC equal to 10 + the creature's CR (rounded up). On a success, you retrieve a usable body part.

Once you harvest a usable part, that corpse can't be used again for any other purpose: you can't craft a stitchwork minion from it, raise it with _animate dead_, or harvest additional parts. The corpse is completely expended.

#### Grafting parts

You can graft a harvested part onto a stitchwork minion of the same size or one size smaller. The maximum number of grafts per minion is determined by Fleshweaving.

Arms, legs, and heads are limited by the anatomy of the corpse used to create the minion. For example, a Humanoid-corpse minion can have up to two arms, two legs, and one head; a Fiend that originally had four arms can provide up to four arms, and an ettin can provide two heads. If the original corpse had fewer limbs, such as a serpentine creature with no legs, the minion inherits that anatomy for grafting purposes. The GM determines these limits.

Tails and wings are exceptions to the graft limit. You can graft a new tail and a pair of wings onto any minion, regardless of the corpse's original anatomy, and they don't count toward its total grafts. The source creature must be the same size as the minion or one size larger. A successfully grafted tail or pair of wings grants the minion the corresponding trait:

- **Tail.** As a bonus action, the minion can make a melee weapon attack with its tail, using your spell attack modifier to hit, with a reach of 10 feet. On a hit, it deals 1d8 + PB bludgeoning damage.
- **Wings.** The minion has a flying speed equal to its walking speed.

To graft a body part, spend 10 minutes using leatherworker's tools and energy from the Mortis Veil to integrate it. Then make an Intelligence (Arcana) or Wisdom (Medicine) check with a DC equal to 10 + half the harvested creature's CR (rounded up). You have advantage if the part is from the same creature type as the minion's original type. If the part comes from a creature with a lower CR than the minion's original body, subtract the difference between their CRs (rounded down, maximum −5) from the check.

On a failed check, you can immediately expend a spell slot to add a bonus to the roll equal to the spell slot's level. The bonus must be enough to turn the failure into a success, and you can use this bonus only once per minion. If grafting is done during a short or long rest, expend the spell slot at the end of the rest. Otherwise, the part doesn't graft but remains viable for one more attempt. After two failed attempts, it is destroyed. A successful graft grants a trait from the table. A grafted part can't be reused.
