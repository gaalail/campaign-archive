# Combat Rules & Actions

Combat in 5e proceeds in rounds, with each round representing approximately 6 seconds of game time.

---

## The Order of Combat

1. **Determine Surprise:** The GM determines if anyone is caught off-guard. Surprised creatures can't move or take actions on their first turn, and can't take reactions until that turn ends.
2. **Establish Positions:** Creatures are placed on the battlefield grid or theatre of the mind.
3. **Roll Initiative:** Every combatant rolls $1\text{d}20 + \text{Dexterity modifier}$. Combatants act in order from highest initiative to lowest.
4. **Take Turns:** Each participant takes their turn in initiative order.
5. **Begin the Next Round:** When everyone has acted, the round ends and a new round begins in the same initiative order.

---

## Actions in Combat

On your turn, you can spend your Action to perform one of the following:

- **Attack:** Make one melee or ranged weapon attack, or multiple attacks if you possess the Extra Attack feature.
- **Cast a Spell:** Cast a cantrip or spell with a casting time of 1 action.
- **Dash:** Gain extra movement for the current turn equal to your speed.
- **Disengage:** Your movement doesn't provoke opportunity attacks for the rest of the turn.
- **Dodge:** Until the start of your next turn, attack rolls against you have disadvantage if you can see the attacker, and you make Dexterity saving throws with advantage.
- **Help:** Give advantage to an ally's next ability check or attack roll against a creature within 5 feet of you.
- **Hide:** Make a Dexterity (Stealth) check in an attempt to become hidden.
- **Ready:** Prepare an action to take later using your Reaction when a specific perceivable trigger occurs.
- **Search:** Devote your attention to finding hidden creatures, traps, or items (Wisdom [Perception] or Intelligence [Investigation]).
- **Use an Object:** Interact with a complex object or a second object during your turn.

---

## Opportunity Attacks

You can make an opportunity attack when a hostile creature that you can see moves out of your reach. To do so, you spend your Reaction to make one melee attack with a weapon right before the creature leaves your reach.
