# Chapter 0: Cosmology & History of Ra Gol'vek

## The Cosmology of Pre-Creation & the First Inversion

Long before Ra Gol'vek, there existed another multiverse, older and vast, one where its Gods had spent aeons shaping every aspect of it to their perception of perfection. They cut out chaos. They cut out passion. They cut out the last threads of chance until the whole structure ran autonomously. Minor Gods were quietly let go; their names simply stopped appearing in the record. Strange peoples at the margins thinned out, generation by generation, until none of them were left to complicate the story. Even magic itself, once wild and alive, was ground down into a gear that turned exactly when it was supposed to.

That precision worked for so long that no one realized what could not possibly be left unaccounted for.

### The Breach

Something slipped through the seam. Small, at first. Nothing in that perfected reality had a word ready for it—every word that didn't fit the machine had been burned out of use long before. It pressed against the world the way a child presses a moth against glass. Without hunger. Without hate.

Gods who had hardened into something closer to law than to beings met that pressure the only way they knew how: by holding firm. A living world might have bent. This one had long forgotten how, so it split down its length instead. Forgotten deities and fever-mad mortals threw themselves into the widening cracks, patching what they could reach. It bought the old multiverse minutes. Maybe hours. Nothing more.

### Excision Under Duress

Twelve of them stopped trying to save the dying multiverse. They started trying to carry a piece of it out instead.

Done right, cutting a reality free from a dying one takes patience. Nobody in that moment had any to spare. The twelve worked the equations half-finished and left tolerances thin that had, otherwise, run infinite. They tore their new world loose and shoved it backward, out of the time the collapse was still dragging everything else through, so whatever was unmaking the old world would have nothing left to follow.

The center held. The edges did not hold as clean—like cutting a square from a burning quilt: saving the pattern in the middle and finishing the border with whatever thread remained in hand when the fire reached them. Solid in the middle; raw, permanently, at the rim.

### The Fracture and the Planar Split

As the twelve stepped into their new world to start shaping it—laying ground and sky over the bones of the laws they'd stolen—the pull of their own creation dragged something in behind them. A fragment of the same horror that had broken the old multiverse open came through the cut, trailing them like smoke through a closing door.

There was nowhere left to run from it. The twelve turned and fought at the exact line between the two realities: divine will grinding against something that had never had a will to grind back with. Eleven of them were unmade so completely there was nothing left to bury—no name held together long enough to carve into stone.

One survived, though not cleanly. Hurt past any healing the old world knew how to give, the last architect drowned in that black flood, and something climbed back out built from a chorus of dying wills twisted together at the exact second they stopped being anything at all. She called herself **[[campaigns/ra-golvek/pantheon/A'aeshtar, the First Mother|A'aeshtar, the First Mother]]** and claimed the black blood filling that new ocean as hers. In the same moment, a new law—the first law—dropped over the infant world like frost: the **Law of Recurrence**, the last wish of twelve dying architects that whatever happened here, death would loop back toward rebirth from now on.

As the young world grew to fill the space it had stolen, it pushed A'aeshtar and her black tide down under everything being born above her. What remained of that first violence split in two:

- Everything born from prayer and the memory of a life worth living gathered into the **Dreamscape**, the **Faewylds**, and the **Positive Energy Plane**.
- Everything born from watching a world end in despair hardened into the **Plane of Shadow**—the anchor that holds time steady—and **the Wound** itself, the scar left behind by the old multiverse.

Ra Gol'vek was built from both halves at once.

## The Era of the Godly Wars

### The Drakonmachy & Early Foundations

The newborn Gods rose from templates stored deep in the roots of the World Tree, already reaching for territory. When they looked out at the Prime Material plane for the first time, ancient elemental powers already held it—chief among them the **Primordial Dragons** formed from positive energy clashing with the elements.

Centuries of war—the **Drakonmachy**—ground both sides down before the dragons finally lost. Survivors withdrew to remote corners of the world, breaking trust between dragon and God for good. Blood soaked into the wild magic earth, birthing serpentine bloodlines, while the fallen rivers of Ichor solidified the first leylines.

During this era in the elemental planes, raw divine energy coalesced into shapes too vast for the world to easily hold: **Titans** first, then **Elder Giants**, and finally the giantkin races. The magnitude of the Titans' essence forged the foundations of the **Astral Plane**.

### The Kingdoms of Night & Fey Alterations

Before planar boundaries fully set, the Gods experimented. **[[campaigns/ra-golvek/pantheon/Raz'a Noct & the Kingdoms of Night|Raz'a Noct]]**, a God of night, freely gave a mortal bloodline his own essence, and charmed fellow deities poured blessing upon blessing onto her children. Instead of contentment, an unfillable hunger opened in her, and she began consuming the Gods' other creations.

The Gods cursed her line: no afterlife would open for them, and no God would ever look on them again beneath open sun. Unwilling to leave his children alone under that sentence, Raz'a Noct tore the Prime Material away from the Plane of Shadow with his own hands, creating the **Kingdoms of Night**. Denied sun and afterlife, her descendants became the first **Pure-Blooded Vampires**.

Elsewhere, fey hands reached across the border and pulled early mortal creations into the wild magic of the **Faewylds**, shaping the **Aelves** and several other fey-claimed peoples.

### The Faerie Expansion & Heaven's Sundering

Finding the Prime Material too small, the Gods launched the **Faerie Expansion**, tearing territory directly from the Faewylds. Every mile gained came over ground wet with fey blood.

In the middle of that campaign, **Heaven** broke open. **Samael** led a rebellion straight into the celestial realms; years of war ended with enough force to shatter the map of creation. A fragment of Heaven tore loose and plunged downward, sealing Samael and his followers inside **Hell**, while the impact ripped open the **Godless Rift** with the **Abyss** waiting at its bottom.

### The Titanomachy & The Gigantomachy

Sensing weakness from Heaven's wound, the **Titans** struck. For the first time, the pantheons needed one another to survive—led in alliance by **Athena**, **Tyr**, and **Heka**. Dozens of primordial deities were extinguished, replaced by younger Gods claiming war, law, and open sky. Beneath the fighting, divine blood soaked into mountain bedrock, from which the first **Dwarves** dug themselves free.

The Titans' fall left an opening seized by the **Giants** under titan-blooded **Thanes** allied with surviving **True Dragons**. During the **Gigantomachy**, Thanes hunted down nature and harvest deities until protective and survivalist Gods rose to replace them. Only after the giant wars burned out did air elementals cross peacefully into the mortal world to build the sky-cities of the **Silfides**.

### The Deumachy: The War of Templates

At last, the Gods turned on each other. Every pantheon fought every other for land, followers, and existence itself. Hundreds of fringe and minor deities were wiped out cleanly, while overlapping survivors merged or slew rivals until what remained were the distinct pantheons known in Ra Gol'vek today: **Daidōis**, **Graikorioi**, **Metu Neter**, **Norse**, **Omneris**, **Plia Dral**, and **Shaivi**.

## The Mortal Era & the Age of Heroes

### The Fall of Sumar

While the Deumachy raged, a well-born priestess in **Sumar** began trading small favors with minor devils before reaching higher to an **Asura**—paying with her High Priest's heart and taking her second name: **Faustus**. Playing opposing infernal courts against one another while quietly forging her own legion of Archdevils (**Adrammelech**, **Asmodeus**, **Belial**, **Belphegor**, **Mammon**, **Mephistopheles**, and **Pythael**), she staged a public confession at Sumar's high temple.

When weakened deities gathered to judge her, Faustus collapsed the ground beneath the temple into **Hell** itself, tearing divinity from a swath of the Mesopotamian pantheon in one afternoon (claiming Belphegor in the fighting as well). For the first time, a mortal alone had brought down Gods.

### The Mortalimachy & The Entente of Divine Concordance

Terror ended the Deumachy within a season, turning divine fear into the century-long **Mortalimachy**. Counter-magic circles turned divine strikes back doubled; mortal champions dubbed **Archlords** rose and fell across generations until counter-magic could be carried by individual soldiers and losing became too costly for the Gods to sustain.

At a crowded peace table led on the mortal side by **Heyl'el**—now remembered as the **Oath-King**, first of the Archlords—and on the divine side by **Athena**, **Tyr**, and **Heka**, both sides ratified the **Entente of Divine Concordance**:

1. **Sovereign Withdrawal:** The pantheons retreated to bridged **Deitric Realms** rather than walking the Prime Material unrestrained; direct intervention requires council approval.
2. **Mortal Rights:** Mortals gained guaranteed freedom of worship (or non-worship) and protection from divine slavery.
3. **Mutual Defense & Sunset Clause:** Both sides swore mutual defense against external cosmic threats under the **Council of Pantheons**—bound to a **two-thousand-year expiration date**.

### Typhon's Gift

Furious at the Hellenes' withdrawal, **Gaia** forged **Typhon** to hunt the weakened Olympians. Trapped beneath a mountain by **Zeus** with the aid of **Nike** and **Thor**, Typhon still endures intact; eight of his nine hearts remain scattered across the world.

## The Modern Landscape: Year 1199 AMH

Twelve centuries after the Entente, Ra Gol'vek answers largely to itself:

- **Arkigike, the Jewel of Omathu:** Seven/nine spire-citadels ruled by the Archlords of the High Council and built upon *Hey-lel Crystal Steel and Stone*.
- **Neocropoli (Ulta):** Artifex Voda's hextech rail-city exporting War-Forged constructs, now stirred by the awakened general **Titanus 87** questioning construct autonomy.
- **Grands Bois Rougeoyants:** The twilight canopy capital of the **Aelves**, diplomatic toward Arkigike yet remembering the Faerie Expansion.
- **Pozolochen Gora:** Dwarven **Lithomancers** beneath the Throat of Titans guarding rumors of stones predating Ra Gol'vek's present cycle.
- **Ayineawa:** Never a signatory to Heyl'el's table—keeping blood rites atop **Maq'tara**, living spells inside **Quetzali**, and the **Pit of Teotli** to the underworld.
- **The Church of the Archangels:** Born on the fallen-Heaven scar left during the Sundering, guided by Prime-tied Archangels outside any Deitric realm.

### The Looming Convergence

Now, past the edges of the map—from the uncharted dark of **Nyar'cath** to the mutated **Hexcrag Isles**—the leylines pulse erratically. Old prophecies speak of the **Obscured** stirring beneath and of the **Law of Recurrence** testing its locks. Behind Exoria's ancient wards, **Ares** and his exiles strain against age-worn seals; in Malebolge, **Faustus** remains as patient as ever; and scholars calculating the Entente's 2,000-year sunset quietly refuse to speak the remaining count aloud.

Meanwhile, a persistent whisper runs from **Exoria** to **Arkigike**: legends of heroes pulled back from death's doorstep, severed from destiny itself, standing on the threshold of a convergence that may break—or reset—Ra Gol'vek forever.
